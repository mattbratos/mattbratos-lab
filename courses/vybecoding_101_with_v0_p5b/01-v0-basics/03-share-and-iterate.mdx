In this chapter, you will learn how to **save**, **rename**, and **share** your project, and how to **improve** it step by step.

## Learning objectives

After this chapter, you will be able to:

- Rename and save your v0 project
- Share it with a link or within your workspace
- Make small, focused improvements over time
- Use feedback to guide new prompts

## Step 1 Rename and save your project

1. In the top bar of the v0 editor, find your **project name**.  
   It might be something generic like `Untitled project`.
2. Click the name and change it to:  
   `VybeCoding 101 Landing Page` (or any name you like).
3. Confirm that v0 shows it as **saved** (often an icon or word like “Saved”).

If autosave is on, v0 will save changes as you work. Still, it’s good practice to check.

## Step 2 Go back to the project list

1. Look for a **Back**, **Projects**, or **Home** button.
2. Click it to return to your **project list**.
3. Confirm that you can see your project with its new name.

You’ve now confirmed that your work is stored and easy to find later.

## Step 3 Open sharing options

Open your project again, then look in the top bar or a menu for a **Share** option.

Typical share options might include:

- **Copy link** to your project
- **Invite collaborators** by email or username
- **Change visibility** (for example, private, team-only, or public)

Because v0’s design can change over time, the exact labels may differ. Look for words like **Share**, **Invite**, or a small **link icon**.

## Step 4 Share a view link (if available)

If v0 supports sharing a view link:

1. Click **Share**.
2. Choose an option like **Copy link** or **Share preview**.
3. Paste this link in a note, email, or chat to someone you trust.

Let them know this is your **first v0 page** and you are learning, so feedback is welcome but perfection is not the goal.

If your version of v0 doesn’t support simple view links yet, you can still:

- Take a **screenshot** of your page
- Copy parts of the text
- Export code (if you are working with a developer)

## Step 5 Ask for simple feedback

Good feedback is **specific** and **kind**. When you share your page, you can ask:

- Is the **main heading** clear about what this page is?
- Does the **button text** make you want to click?
- Is anything **confusing** or **hard to read**?

Make a short list (2–3 items) you want to improve based on any feedback you get.

## Step 6 Iterate with small changes

Open your project again and make a few focused improvements.

Examples of small iterations:

- **Clarify text**  
  If someone said the title is unclear, rewrite it:
  
  ```text
  Learn v0 from zero in one friendly mini-course.
  ```

- **Adjust contrast**  
  If text is hard to read, change background or text color for better contrast.

- **Improve layout** with a prompt  
  Use a follow-up prompt like:

  ```text
  Make the hero section more compact and move the call to action above the fold.
  ```

Apply one change at a time, then review the result.

## Step 7 Save a new "version" using history or duplicates

To keep track of progress, you can:

- Use **History** (if available) to see past versions
- **Duplicate** the project before big changes, giving it a new name like:
  - `VybeCoding 101 Landing Page v1`
  - `VybeCoding 101 Landing Page v2`

This way you can compare versions and learn what worked better.

## A simple iteration loop

Here’s a mini “loop” you can repeat whenever you use v0:

1. **Prompt**  
   Describe what you want.
2. **Generate**  
   Let v0 create or update the page.
3. **Review**  
   Look at what you like and what feels off.
4. **Adjust**  
   Edit text, styles, or sections.
5. **Share**  
   Get feedback from someone else.
6. **Repeat**  
   Use new prompts guided by that feedback.

Over time, this loop will make you much more comfortable with **both design and v0 itself**.

## When are you "done"?

You are done **for now** when:

- Your page has a **clear title**, **description**, and **button**
- You’re able to **open** the project, **edit** it, and **share** it
- You’ve made at least **two rounds of small improvements**

Remember: pages can always be improved later. Don’t wait for perfect.

## Where to go next

After finishing this chapter and the module, you can:

- Try building a different page (for a hobby, product, or event)
- Explore v0 templates and tweak them
- Learn more about the code v0 generates (if you’re curious about development)

Most importantly, keep **experimenting** with prompts and designs.
