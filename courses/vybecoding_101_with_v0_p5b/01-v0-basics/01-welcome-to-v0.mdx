In this chapter, you will **sign in to v0** and learn what you see on the screen: where to type prompts, how to view results, and how projects are organized.

## Learning objectives

After this chapter, you will be able to:

- Open v0 in your browser and sign in
- Understand the **home screen** and **project list**
- Recognize the **prompt panel** and **preview area**
- Know where basic controls like **Run**, **History**, and **Settings** are

## Step 1 Open v0 and sign in

1. Open your browser.
2. Go to the v0 website (for example: `https://v0.dev`).  
   (If the exact URL changes, search for **"v0 vercel"**.)
3. Click **Sign in** or **Get started**.
4. Choose a sign-in method:
   - **GitHub** (common for developers), or
   - **Email** (you may get a magic link to your inbox).
5. Follow the on-screen steps until you see the **v0 home screen**.

If you see a list of projects and a button like **New** or **Create**, you’re in the right place.

## Step 2 Explore the home screen

On the home screen, you will usually see:

- A **New project** button  
  Use this to start building something from scratch.
- A **list of recent projects**  
  This shows what you or your team have already created.
- Maybe some **templates** or **examples**  
  These are pre-built pages you can open and explore.

Take a moment to click around. You won’t break anything.

## Step 3 Create a blank project

1. Click **New project** (or a similar button like **New** or **Create**).
2. If v0 asks you to choose a template, pick **Blank** (or the simplest option).
3. Wait a few seconds while v0 prepares your new project.

You should now see the **main v0 editor**.

## Step 4 Learn the editor layout

The editor is usually split into a few main areas:

- **Prompt panel** (often on the left or at the bottom)  
  - This is where you **type what you want**, like “Create a simple landing page for a music festival.”  
  - You’ll also see the **Run** button here.

- **Preview area** (main center area)  
  - This shows the **page that v0 generates**.  
  - You can click on text, buttons, and sections to select them.

- **Right sidebar / inspector**  
  - When you select an element in the preview, this area lets you **change properties**: text, colors, spacing, etc.

- **Top bar**  
  - Usually shows your **project name**, **Save** status, and sometimes **Share** or **Settings**.

Look at each part and say out loud (or in your head) what it is for. This helps you remember.

## Step 5 Basic controls to know

Find and note these controls in your v0 editor:

- **Prompt input**  
  The big text box or area where you’ll describe what you want.

- **Run / Generate** button  
  Makes v0 read your prompt and update the page.

- **Undo / Redo** buttons  
  Let you go back or forward if you make a mistake.

- **History** (if available)  
  Shows past versions or generations.

- **Preview mode** vs **Edit mode** (if shown)  
  - In **Edit mode**, you can click elements and change things.
  - In **Preview mode**, you see the page more like a real visitor.

You do not need to memorize every icon today. Just know where the main **prompt**, **Run**, and **preview** areas are.

## Mini practice explore the interface

Try this quick exercise:

1. Click once in the **prompt panel** and type:  
   `I am testing v0 and learning the interface.`
2. Do **not** click Run yet. Just notice how it feels to type there.
3. Click on the **preview area** (even if it’s empty or simple).
4. Try selecting any text or section you see.
5. Look at the **right sidebar** and see what changes when different things are selected.

You just practiced the basic moves you’ll use all the time.

## Common questions

### Do I need to understand the code v0 generates?

No. For now, treat v0 like a **visual builder**. You can learn about the code later if you want.

### What if my screen looks a bit different?

v0 is updated often. Layouts and labels can change slightly.  
If you don’t see something exactly as described, look for **similar words or icons** (for example, "Run" vs "Generate").

### Can I break something?

Not really. You can always:

- Undo changes
- Close the project and reopen it
- Start a new project if things feel messy
